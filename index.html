HomeDiary

Full-stack web app

Track your home's history and documents

HomeDiary is a private diary for your home. Sign in, add your properties, and log a timeline of everything that happens to them: purchase, damage, repairs, inspections, and more, each with photos and documents attached. Paperwork like purchase documents, insurance policies, and warranties gets its own place for each property, and every account only ever sees its own homes. The app has a typed REST API with JWT cookie auth and bcrypt password hashing, and uploads go straight to S3-compatible object storage. It runs as a single Docker service on Render.

Kachun Quizer

AI study app

Photo your notes, get quizzed on them

Take a photo of your notes or paste them in, and Kachun Quizer turns them into an Anki-style flashcard deck. Tesseract.js reads the text out of the photo in the browser. Claude then writes real comprehension questions from it, through a Cloudflare Worker that keeps the API key off the client. If that request fails, the app falls back to an offline generator that uses NLP to blank out the key word in each sentence, so a study session never hits a dead end. Each deck uses spaced repetition: facts you miss come back sooner, and facts you know show up less often.

MathPop

Math game for kids

Multiplication, addition, subtraction

MathPop is a sibling to PitchPop that uses arithmetic instead of chords. Kids pick multiplication, addition, subtraction, or a random mix, then solve five-problem rounds with big colored cards, chimes, and confetti. They can tap an answer or write it by hand: a small neural network I trained on MNIST, with augmentation to handle messy kid handwriting, reads the digits right in the browser. The wrong answer choices come from common slip-ups, like being off by one factor or adding instead of multiplying, so getting it right means knowing the answer. Problems are read aloud with Piper TTS, and progress is saved on the device and broken down by operation.

PitchPop

Music game for kids

Learn perfect pitch by colored chords

PitchPop started as a game we play at home: I play a chord on the piano, and my kids grab the matching color flag and shout it out. The app turns that into a quiz. Tap the rainbow to hear one of nine chords, pick its color from four choices, and get confetti and spoken feedback when you're right. The piano sound is synthesized from harmonics with the Web Audio API, so the app ships no audio samples. The voice is Piper, a neural text-to-speech model that runs in the browser through ONNX Runtime. There's also a free-play mode, plus a progress page for each child that tracks accuracy and which colors have come up. It installs to a phone's home screen as a PWA.

  • Vite
  • Web Audio API

PowerPay

Money20/20 Hackathon, Las Vegas

Pay online by digital check

Card payments cost merchants about 2.9% per transaction, and paying straight from a bank account online usually fails because there's no real-time check that the payment is good.
